## Essence
Desirable difficulties (Robert Bjork) are practice conditions that feel harder and slower and lower performance during practice, yet improve long-term retention and transfer, as long as the learner can still succeed with effort.

## Propositions
- P1. Spacing, retrieval and interleaving share a pattern.
- P2. They make practice feel harder and slower, and they make performance during practice worse.
- P3. They improve long-term retention and transfer to new problems.
- P4. The psychologist Robert Bjork called such conditions desirable difficulties.
- P5. A difficulty is desirable only if the learner can still succeed with effort.
- P6. A task that is too hard leads to failure without learning.

## Intuition and contrasts
- Intuition: The extra effort is what builds the memory, but only if the effort can pay off.
- Contrast: Not every difficulty is desirable: a task that is too hard for the learner to succeed with effort produces failure without learning.
