## Essence
Trying to recall information from memory strengthens that memory more than reading it again.

## Propositions
- P1. Retrieval practice (the testing effect) means trying to bring information out of memory instead of taking it in again.
- P2. Answering a question, writing down everything you remember, and explaining an idea without notes are all forms of retrieval.
- P3. In a typical experiment, students read a short text and then either reread it or wrote down what they could recall, without feedback.
- P4. On a test one week later the recall group remembered more than the rereading group.
- P5. The rereading group had felt more confident, so confidence did not predict the better result.

## Intuition and contrasts
- Intuition: Pulling a memory out is a stronger workout for it than looking at it again.
- Contrast: Retrieval practice is not the same as rereading: both involve the text, but only retrieval requires producing the content from memory.
