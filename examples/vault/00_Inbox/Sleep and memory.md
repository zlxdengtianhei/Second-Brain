# Sleep and memory

Sleep after learning helps keep what was learned. During sleep, and especially during deep slow-wave sleep, the brain replays recent experiences. Memories that first depended on the hippocampus become more stable and better connected to older knowledge. This process is called consolidation.

In experiments, people who sleep after learning a list of word pairs remember more of them the next day than people who stay awake for the same length of time. The benefit is not only rest: the amount of slow-wave sleep is related to how much is kept.

Missing sleep before learning hurts too. After a night without sleep, people form fewer new memories the next day. For a student this means that a night of sleep between studying and the test is part of studying, not time taken away from it.
