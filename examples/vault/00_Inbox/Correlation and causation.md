# Correlation is not causation

Two variables are correlated when they tend to move together. A correlation between A and B can arise because A causes B, because B causes A, because a third factor causes both, or by chance. A third factor that drives both variables is called a confounder. Ice cream sales and drowning deaths rise together in summer, but neither causes the other: hot weather drives both.

To find out whether A causes B, a randomised controlled trial assigns A at random. Random assignment balances confounders between the groups on average, including confounders nobody thought to measure. When an experiment is impossible or unethical, researchers adjust for the confounders they measured, but confounders they did not measure can still mislead them.
